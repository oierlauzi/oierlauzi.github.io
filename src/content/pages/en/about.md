I grew up in Gernika-Lumo, in Bizkaia, and now live in Madrid. I am a PhD
student here, at the Biocomputing Unit of the Centro Nacional de Biotecnología
(CNB-CSIC). My work is on image processing methods for Cryo-EM and Cryo-ET. A
“la Caixa” Foundation INPhINIT fellowship supports it.

I joined the unit two years before the PhD, as technical staff. The job was
maintaining the Xmipp3 package and looking after its compute nodes and
workstations. Before that I studied at the Universidad Politécnica de Madrid.
I took a degree in Sound and Image Engineering first, then two master's degrees,
in Telecommunications Engineering and in Electronic Systems Engineering.

I did not come to structural biology from biology, but from video and computer
graphics. My bachelor's thesis was a software video mixer called *Cenital*. It
was built on *Zuazo*, a real-time and modular video processing library I had
written in my spare time as an undergraduate. Those interests never really left.
The work I enjoy most still brings together high-performance computing, computer
graphics and image processing. I am happiest with a problem that has a geometric
analogue, something I can picture and not only compute.

Outside work I am a railway enthusiast. I volunteer at the Asociación de Amigos
del Ferrocarril de Madrid, helping where I can to preserve and run the *Tren de
los 80*. Vintage computers and electronics are another weakness, mostly 90s RISC
workstations. So I keep a small collection of Silicon Graphics machines and
still tinker with them. They can even run the structural biology software of
their day.
